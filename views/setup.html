Before You ScanSETUP

Sixty seconds of setup buys you a diagnostic image. Probe, preset, depth, gain — in that order, every time.

The sixty-second setup

  1. Pick the probe for the depth you need, then select the matching preset — the preset changes the frame rate and the orientation convention.
  2. Confirm orientation before you interpret anything: tap the marker side of the probe and watch which edge of the screen brightens.
  3. Set depth so the structure of interest fills the lower two-thirds, then set gain so fluid is black and tissue is gray.
  4. Freeze, scroll back through the cine loop, and measure on the best frame — never on the live image.

Don’t be fooled — pitfalls

  • Interpreting before confirming orientationLeft and right reverse between presets. One finger tap on the probe marker tells you which screen edge is which — do it every time you change probe or preset.
  • Too much depthThe commonest reason a bedside image looks unreadable. Shrink depth until the target fills the screen.
  • Gain used as a fix for a bad windowGain makes noise brighter, not anatomy clearer. Reposition the patient first.
  • Measuring on a live imageFreeze and scroll. Cardiac measurements are phase-specific and cannot be trusted mid-motion.
  • Scanning without a questionPOCUS answers a specific yes/no. Decide the question before the gel goes on, and document what you asked and what you saw.

Bad image? Fix it

  • Dark, grainy image in a large patientLower the frequency, switch to curvilinear or phased array, and increase output before touching gain.
  • Bright near field, dark far fieldAdjust TGC — pull near-field gain down and far-field gain up rather than moving overall gain.
  • Ribs everywhereRotate to run along the intercostal space, or move one space up or down. Reposition the patient before you fight the anatomy.
  • Patient cannot cooperateTake short clips through several cycles and review them frozen; a five-second clip beats a perfect live image you cannot hold.
  • Not enough contactMore gel, firmer even pressure, and check the probe is flush — air under an edge kills a third of the image.

So what — what the finding changes

  • The image will not become diagnosticSay so. A documented inadequate study is a legitimate result; an over-read bad image is not.
  • The finding will change managementConfirm it in a second view or a second modality before acting on it.
  • Between patientsClean the probe per local policy, wipe the cable, and use a cover for anything near a wound or mucosa.
