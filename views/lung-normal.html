Get the view

  1. Hold the probe longitudinally, perpendicular to the ribs: two rib shadows with the pleural line between them is the bat sign.
  2. Center the pleural line and reduce depth until it occupies the top third of the screen.
  3. Watch through one full breath for shimmer along the pleura — that is lung sliding.
  4. Then work the zones in order: anterior upper, anterior lower, lateral, and posterolateral at the base.

What you are looking at

Soft tissue and rib
Layered muscle and fascia in the near field; ribs cast clean posterior shadows — the wings of the bat.
Pleural line
The bright horizontal line where parietal meets visceral pleura — the reference for everything else on the screen.
Lung sliding
Shimmering to-and-fro motion along that line with respiration. Present means no pneumothorax at that spot.
A-lines
Equidistant horizontal repeats of the pleural line — normal reverberation through air.
Seashore sign
The M-mode signature of sliding: grainy sand under a smooth sky. Side-by-side on the pneumothorax page.

Don’t be fooled — pitfalls

  • Subcutaneous emphysema abolishes everythingAir in the soft tissue blocks the beam and mimics pneumothorax. If the near field is a mess of bright noise, do not interpret the pleura.
  • Lung pulse is not slidingCardiac motion transmitted to the pleura shows movement without true sliding — think atelectasis or mainstem intubation, not pneumothorax.
  • Scanning below the diaphragmAt the bases it is easy to image liver or spleen and call it consolidated lung. Find the diaphragm and stay above it.
  • Too much depthDeep settings shrink the pleural line to a few pixels and hide the detail you are looking for.

Bad image? Fix it

  • No bat signYou are parallel to a rib. Rotate 90° so the footprint crosses two ribs.
  • Cannot tell if it is slidingFreeze the debate with M-mode: seashore is sliding, barcode is not.
  • Noisy near fieldReduce overall gain, drop near-field TGC, and prefer the linear probe for pleural detail.
  • Need to see effusion or consolidation tooSwitch to curvilinear or phased array at 10–12 cm — the linear probe will not reach.

So what — what the finding changes

  • A-profile with sliding, in a hypoxic patientCardiogenic pulmonary edema becomes unlikely and pneumothorax is excluded at the spots you scanned — pivot to COPD, asthma, or PE.
  • A-profile without slidingA-lines alone do not rule out pneumothorax — they persist in PTX too. Absent sliding is the finding that matters; go hunt for a lung point.
  • A-profile in undifferentiated shockLungs are not the answer. Move to the heart, IVC, abdomen and aorta.
  • Bilateral A-lines after intubationConfirms aerated lung, but check for equal sliding — absent sliding on the left suggests mainstem placement.
